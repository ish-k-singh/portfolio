Oura filed its S-1 confidentially on May 21, 2026, carrying an $11B valuation. Their pitch to investors: they're not a wearable device anymore. They're an AI health platform. With a mission to make health a daily practice, the company has expanded far beyond its original promise — sleep, activity, and readiness — into a sprawling suite of AI-powered features.

I've worn my Oura ring for about five months. I thought I knew the product. But while compiling this report, I discovered features I didn't know existed.

If Oura's growth story depends on AI engagement, are users actually showing up for it?

Research Question

As Oura expands its AI-powered feature set ahead of its IPO, are users actually engaging with AI-driven features like Oura Advisor — or are they anchoring to the same surface-level metrics they've always used, and why?

  • Which features do users engage with daily versus occasionally versus never?
  • Do users distinguish between passive AI (Readiness Score) and active AI (Oura Advisor)?
  • Where does UI complexity or lack of awareness create drop-off?
  • Has Oura's AI actually changed user behavior in any meaningful way?

Methodology

How This Study Was Conducted

This study uses a mixed-methods approach — combining broad secondary research with targeted qualitative interviews to triangulate behavioral patterns at scale and deepen them with direct user insight.

Primary Research

  • 4 semi-structured interviews, 30–40 min each
  • Think-aloud protocol with live app walkthrough
  • Mix of new users (<6 months) and long-term users (1+ years)
  • Intentional gender mix given Oura's women's health focus

Secondary Research

  • r/ouraring — thematic analysis of community posts
  • App Store reviews — filtered by rating and recency
  • Trustpilot — critical review analysis
  • Industry data and press coverage

Finding 01
The Passive AI Is Trusted. The Active AI Is Invisible.

Every participant anchored their daily routine on the same two features: Readiness Score and Sleep Score. Not because they lacked curiosity — but because those features require nothing. Everything else requires effort most users never spend.

Three of four participants either didn't know Oura Advisor existed or stumbled on it by accident.

"I didn't even know that was a thing until just now. Nobody told me and I've never stumbled across it in the app."

Margaret, 6 months

"Never used it, wasn't really aware it existed."

Derek, 3 years

What's striking is that three participants independently reached for the same metaphor — without prompting and without hearing each other's answers.

"Automatic is like the app texting you. Finding stuff is like having to call someone. I'm a texter."

Jordan, 8–9 months

Same observation. Three people. Zero coordination. That kind of unprompted convergence suggests this isn't a personal preference — it's a structural product problem.

Finding 02
When the AI Works, It Changes Behavior — But Only When It's Automatic.

All four participants had a genuine behavior change story. Alex cut his afternoon coffee. Margaret started taking lunch walks. Jordan put her phone across the room. Derek restructured his entire travel routine. Every single one of these changes came from passive data surfaced automatically — nobody sought it out.

"It stopped telling me what I should do and just showed me the data until I figured it out myself."

Derek, 3 years

The most powerful AI moments in this study were the ones users didn't notice were AI at all.

Finding 03
Users Don't Think of Oura as an AI Product.

When asked directly, all four said health tracker first. Not one arrived at AI unprompted.

"Health tracker for sure. I didn't even know it had AI features until this conversation."

Margaret, 6 months

"I think of it as a health tracker that's slowly becoming an AI product but isn't fully there yet."

Alex, 2 years

Oura is telling investors it's an AI health platform. Its most engaged users are describing it as a fancy sleep tracker. That gap surfaces during public market scrutiny.

Finding 04
Discovery Is Broken.

Oura Labs was completely unknown to both Margaret and Derek. Cycle Insights had been set up and abandoned by both female participants. Cardiovascular Age had been checked once and never revisited. Oura Advisor had a 75% unaided awareness failure rate in this sample.2

"It took me watching three YouTube videos to feel oriented. Nobody tells you where to start."

Jordan, 8–9 months

"Anything beyond the automatic stuff feels like being handed a textbook and told to figure it out myself."

Margaret, 6 months

For a company whose subscription value depends on users finding its deeper features, broken discovery isn't just a UX problem — it's a retention and revenue problem.

Finding 05
One Product, Four Completely Different Needs.

Alex wanted HRV front and center. Margaret wanted a weight-loss mode. Jordan wanted better onboarding. Derek wanted push notifications that briefed him in two sentences. Oura's one-size-fits-all interface isn't serving any of them perfectly.

"The whole app feels designed for people who already have healthy habits. I'm still trying to build the habits. That's a really different starting point."

Margaret, 6 months

"There's an enormous amount of untapped value in that app that most people will never access. That's not a user failure — that's a product design challenge."

Alex, 2 years
Secondary Research

To validate what emerged from interviews, I analyzed r/ouraring, App Store reviews, and Trustpilot — looking for patterns, not complaints. Two themes emerged consistently across all three platforms.

Theme 1: The App's Expanding UI Is Creating Navigation Fatigue

Reddit

Theme 2: Active AI Features Aren't Delivering on Their Promise

App Store

The subscription tension sits underneath both themes. When users can't find or don't get value from active AI features behind the paywall, the subscription stops making sense. That's not a pricing problem — it's a discovery and delivery problem.

Synthesis

What It All Means

Four participants. Four different demographics and reasons for buying the ring. Through the research, the same patterns kept surfacing: anchoring behaviors, invisible features, and the gap between what Oura is building and what users are actually experiencing.

Before drawing conclusions, it was worth asking: is this just four people, or does it reflect something broader? To find out, I turned to secondary research — systematically analyzing community forums, app store reviews, and social media to see whether the patterns held at scale.

They did.

Oura has built something users genuinely love — but what they love is a narrow slice of what Oura has actually built. The passive experience is trusted, habit-forming, and in many cases life-changing. The active AI layer is largely invisible, misunderstood, or tried once and abandoned.

This isn't a story about a bad product. It's a story about a product whose ambitions have outpaced its ability to bring users along. Oura added 14 new features in 2025 alone.3 Users are still navigating to the same two metrics they checked on day one.

The gap matters more now than it ever has. Investors will eventually ask whether users are engaging with the AI. Based on this research, the honest answer is: most aren't. Not because they don't want to — but because nobody showed them where it was.

Key Insights

  1. Users trust Oura's AI most when they don't know it's AI — every behavior change in this study came from passive data, never from an active AI feature.
  2. Oura Advisor exists in a discovery dead zone — 3 of 4 participants either didn't know it existed or found it by accident.
  3. Feature expansion is outpacing user mental models — users are still navigating to the same two metrics they checked on day one.
  4. When active AI is found, it often disappoints — users described responses that felt generic and disconnected from their actual goals.
  5. The subscription feels unjustified to users who never find the AI — the complaint isn't price, it's invisible value.
Recommendations

What Oura Should Consider

These recommendations are framed for Oura's product team — focused on what to solve, not prescribing exactly how.

01
Surface Oura Advisor within the natural morning check-in flow.

Users are already in the app every morning. Advisor shouldn't require navigation — it should meet users where they already are.

02
Build a guided discovery experience for new features.

Oura is shipping features faster than users can find them. A lightweight onboarding moment each time a significant feature launches would close that gap without a full redesign.

03
Clarify the distinction between passive insights and active AI tools.

Users don't currently understand that some features require their participation. Making that visible would set better expectations and drive more intentional engagement.

04
Personalize the home screen by user goal.

Margaret needed a weight loss lens. Derek needed a two-sentence briefing. Alex needed HRV front and center. A goal-based home screen is the difference between a product that feels made for you and one that feels made for someone else.

The Bigger Picture

What This Means for Health AI

Oura's IPO story is built on a single premise: that it has evolved from a sleep tracker into an AI health platform. This research suggests that premise is real — but it's running ahead of the user experience that would make it true in practice.

Apple has reportedly slowed its AI health coach plans until the technology is useful enough to deliver real value.4 Samsung, Whoop, and Garmin are all racing toward AI-powered insights. The question every company in this space is quietly asking is the same one this research set out to answer: are users actually engaging with health AI, or are they just wearing the hardware?

"Solving it doesn't require building something new. It requires making what already exists impossible to miss."

Based on this research, the answer for Oura is nuanced. Users are engaging — deeply, meaningfully, in ways that are genuinely changing their behavior. But they're engaging with the AI they don't know is AI. The layer they do know about remains largely untouched. That gap is Oura's most important product problem right now.

Methodology Reflection

Limitations & What Comes Next

Four interviews is not a statistically representative sample — findings are directional, not definitive. The secondary research skews toward vocal users, since people who post on Reddit and leave app store reviews are not the average Oura member. Without access to Oura's internal usage analytics, behavioral patterns are inferred from self-report and community observation rather than measured directly.

What this study can't tell you is exactly how many users have opened Oura Advisor, how long they stayed, or whether they returned. That data exists inside Oura.

The most important version of this research is the one Oura's own team should be running with real engagement data. This study points to where to look. Internal analytics would reveal what's actually there.
